# Twofold user guide

## The mark

The ring, the center and the dots each show one reading; by default battery, Wi-Fi and memory. The ring fills with its value, the center shows a glyph or a number, and the lit dots show the value in quarters. Settings → Indicator picks what goes where, the ring style, thickness, size, and color.

## Unfold

Click the mark and it unfolds into one icon per reading, drawn like the system's own menu bar icons. Click an icon to open its page, or the space between icons for the full list. It folds back when the popover closes, when you click elsewhere, or after 6 seconds.

Settings → General → Unfold switches to On Hover, where the mark unfolds under the pointer and folds when it leaves, or to Never, where a click opens the full list. Right-click always opens the full list.

The battery icon shows its percentage when System Settings → Control Center → Battery → Show Percentage is on.

The unfolded mark is wider than the folded one. On a Mac with a notch, if Twofold is the leftmost menu bar item, it can grow under the notch and macOS hides it. Hold ⌘ and drag the mark to the right of another item.

## The popover

Each row opens a page; `Esc` goes back, and `Esc` on the list closes it. The Wi-Fi icon turns Wi-Fi on and off, and the speaker icon mutes.

- **Wi-Fi** needs Location access to show network names: macOS treats them as location data. Twofold uses it for nothing else. Click a network to join it; leave the password empty for a network your Mac already knows.
- **Bluetooth** asks for permission the first time you open the popover. Click a device to connect or disconnect it. Battery levels appear for keyboards, mice and trackpads; AirPods don't share theirs with other apps.
- **Display** has Keep Awake, which stops the display from sleeping until you turn it off or quit.

Settings → Popover hides and reorders rows. Profiles save the three readings of the mark; with profiles, a switcher appears at the top of the popover. "Add the three starters" adds Work, Travel and Minimal.

## Moments

When something happens — plugging in, a low battery, mute, a new sound output, a Bluetooth device or a drive connecting — the center of the mark briefly changes to its icon, such as your headphones or a drive. Turn it off in Settings → General → Show moments.
